The estate administration roadmap

The usual journey for administering a Muslim estate in Singapore, from the death certificate to the final distribution. It is a practical guide, not a legal framework. Use the PathFinder for a route tailored to your answers.

Was the deceased Muslim?

If so, Faraid (Islamic inheritance law) governs who inherits and in what shares, and an Inheritance Certificate from the Syariah Court is needed.

Was there a Wasiat (Islamic will)?

Yes — the executor applies for a Grant of Probate.No — the next-of-kin applies for Letters of Administration.

Is the estate $50,000 or below?

Excluding CPF and nominated insurance, and with none of the disqualifying factors, the Public Trustee may administer the estate instead of a court application.

Timing. Applications for the Inheritance Certificate and for Letters of Administration or Probate can be filed before the deceased’s debts are settled. What must wait is the distribution: expenses, debts and other liabilities are paid first, any valid Wasiat is given effect, and only the net estate is then distributed under Faraid.

  1. Identify and settle the estate’s liabilities

    Funeral and burial expenses, debts owed to creditors, and outstanding religious liabilities such as unpaid Zakat, Kafarah (expiation payments) or oath penalties. These are paid from the estate before the net estate is distributed — they do not have to be cleared before applying for the Inheritance Certificate or a court grant.

  2. Obtain the Death Certificate

    Once the death is certified by a doctor it is registered automatically with the Immigration & Checkpoints Authority (ICA). The next-of-kin downloads the digital death certificate from MyLegacy@LifeSG. It is needed for post-death matters, including the burial or cremation permit from the National Environment Agency (NEA).

  3. Apply to the Syariah Court for the Inheritance Certificate

    The Inheritance Certificate (IC) identifies all the legal Faraid heirs and their exact fractional shares. For an estimate of the shares, use the Syariah Court’s Online Trial Inheritance Calculator.

  4. Search for and list all estate assets

    Check every institution: HDB, banks, insurance companies, the CPF Board (for CPF savings) and investment entities. The Public Trustee’s Office is only involved where it administers a qualifying estate of $50,000 or below, or distributes un-nominated CPF monies.

  5. Apply for the court grant

    The application is made at the Family Justice Courts. Which grant you apply for depends on whether the deceased left a Wasiat (Islamic will):

    Wasiat: yes — the executor applies for a Grant of Probate.Wasiat: no — the next-of-kin applies for a Grant of Letters of Administration.

  6. Liquidate the assets into cash

    The administrator or executor presents the court order and the Inheritance Certificate to the financial institutions and property agents.

  7. Distribute the net estate in sequence

    Faraid shares to the heirs as set out in the Inheritance Certificate; Wasiat bequests (up to one-third, to non-heirs); Nuzriah obligations; and any unallocated surplus remitted to Baitulmal.

  8. Appeals, if there is a dispute

    If disputes arise over Baitulmal remittances, beneficiaries may submit a formal appeal to MUIS and the Syariah Court.
